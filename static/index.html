Hermes — who can change the code on Base

Every address below was probed directly for its ERC-1967 / EIP-1822 storage slots on Base mainnet, and whatever can upgrade each one (its admin, its beacon, or for UUPS its own access control) was followed through ProxyAdmins, Safes, timelocks, roles and smart accounts to the keys at the bottom, including contracts on Ethereum acting through their L1→L2 alias. Keys is the fewest keys an upgrade takes; "unknown" means the chain was not fully identified, not that it is safe. The graph is kept between scans, so changes are recorded, and any address can be looked up. Nothing here is priced.

Sample. Addresses come from a hand-curated list plus chain-wide discovery of ERC-1967 Upgraded, BeaconUpgraded and AdminChanged events, read in windows spread evenly over Base's history and capped at a few addresses per implementation, beacon or admin. It is a sample of Base, not all of it: without the cap, mass-deployed clones of a handful of contracts would be most of the index.

Coverage. Only ERC-1967 (transparent, UUPS, beacon) and EIP-1822 are detected. Contracts using inherited storage, eternal storage, Diamond/EIP-2535, or the pre-1967 org.zeppelinos slot are indexed but not counted as covered — USDC on Base is one of them. A pattern this scan cannot see is named here rather than silently omitted.
